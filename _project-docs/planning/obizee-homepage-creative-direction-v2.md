# oBizee homepage creative direction — “Every business has a whole story”

Date: 10 September 2026  
Scope: public oBizee website rebuild only  
Status: creative direction for design and implementation

## The correction

The earlier single-business signature demo was too narrow. A generic fictional business would be equally weak because it removes the details that make a product demonstration credible.

The homepage will use a cast of different businesses. Each business demonstrates the oBizee capability that matters most in its world, while one consistent interface and visual language connects them.

- Fashion: a storefront that handles products, sizes and variants.
- Jewellery: payment, packing and order detail.
- Food: a time-sensitive local order and same-day fulfilment.
- Handmade: materials, costing and true profit.
- Local retail: stock movement and customer retention.

This is not five separate mini-sites. It is one story about a system flexible enough to understand different businesses.

## Competitive diagnosis

SitesPlaced has invested in visible abundance. It shows a large gallery, dramatically different template art directions, live stores, product programs, integrations, pricing, AI and a large acquisition surface. It names and packages its capabilities clearly. The volume itself creates confidence.

Its current public homepage also carries many simultaneous promotional messages, plan details and calls to action. Its strongest design work appears in the template catalogue, while the homepage must explain an increasingly broad product universe.

oBizee should meet the standard of visual ambition and visible proof, then use a clearer central idea: the beautiful storefront is only the visible edge of the business. oBizee connects what happens behind it—orders, materials, vendors, fulfilment and money.

The competitive claim will not be “we have more features.” The experience will let the visitor discover a deeper chain of work.

## The idea

### Public thought

**Your business, beautifully connected.**

Supporting thought:

**From the product you sell to the profit you keep, oBizee brings your store, orders, stock, shipping and money together.**

Pricing proof remains visible near the primary action:

**₹0/month. Pay only when you sell—1% per order, capped at ₹10 after ₹50,000 in orders.**

All wording remains subject to final commercial verification.

### Emotional arc

The homepage moves through four feelings:

1. **Recognition:** “This is for a business like mine.”
2. **Desire:** “My store could look this good.”
3. **Discovery:** “It handles much more than the storefront.”
4. **Confidence:** “I understand the cost, I have seen proof, and I know how to begin.”

The page should feel calm at first, expand into a rich product world, become precise around operations and money, and finish with confidence.

## The opening scene

The first viewport is editorial and spacious. It does not try to show an entire dashboard.

Left:
- compact brand/navigation;
- headline and two-line explanation;
- Start free;
- Move my shop;
- one pricing proof line.

Right:
- one large, high-quality storefront canvas;
- a quiet selector: Fashion / Jewellery / Food / Handmade / Local retail;
- selecting a category changes the store identity, product imagery and one supporting operational card;
- the oBizee shell remains constant.

The transitions make the point: the merchant changes; oBizee adapts.

Desktop composition can be asymmetric, with the storefront extending beyond the grid. Mobile shows one selected store with a horizontally scrollable, keyboard-accessible category list. The first screen stays useful without motion.

## Homepage choreography

### Act 1 — “Made for businesses like yours”

A full-width strip introduces five merchant worlds through real, approved oBizee shop imagery. Each receives a concise statement tied to its actual needs:

- “Sizes, colours and stock that stay together.”
- “Every order, payment and package accounted for.”
- “An order today. A delivery today.”
- “Know what every product really costs.”
- “See what is moving before the shelf runs empty.”

The statements are illustrative until matched to verified capabilities and approved examples.

This replaces a generic “Who it is for” icon grid. It demonstrates recognition immediately.

### Act 2 — “A storefront worthy of what you sell”

Show a curated editorial gallery rather than a wall of template cards. Use a few strong storefront compositions across categories, with desktop and mobile views and a clear route to the full gallery.

Each preview includes:
- visual character;
- intended business;
- relevant commerce behavior;
- availability;
- real demo or clearly labelled concept.

The design quality must be visible at actual viewing size. Tiny thumbnails do not establish quality.

### Act 3 — “One order touches everything”

This is the signature product sequence. The active merchant world can change, but the logic remains stable:

1. A product is published.
2. A customer places an order.
3. Stock or materials update.
4. A delivery option is chosen.
5. Costs are accounted for.
6. The merchant sees what remains.

The content panel states the business consequence. The visual panel shows a faithful oBizee interaction or product capture. Users can select any chapter. Scrolling may advance the scene on large screens, but it never removes control or delays reading.

This is where oBizee establishes product depth.

### Act 4 — “Know what you kept”

A simple, transparent profit story follows the order:

Revenue  
− product/material cost  
− packaging  
− delivery  
− payment cost  
− discount/return impact  
= money kept

Use one example at a time and state assumptions. Let the category selector change the nature of the costs. Do not create a fake universal margin.

### Act 5 — “Already selling? Bring it with you”

The existing import flow becomes a major conversion moment. Present multiple supported sources clearly and distinguish live, limited and upcoming sources. The visitor enters a public reference, reviews what oBizee found and decides what happens next.

The before/after is not Instagram-versus-oBizee. It is scattered business information becoming an editable storefront and usable catalogue.

Preserve the current source parsing, URL handoff and authentication rules.

### Act 6 — “Real businesses, real evidence”

Use the strongest approved merchant evidence as an editorial story:
- merchant or business identity;
- shop imagery;
- a specific operational change;
- attributable quote;
- date/context for numerical claims;
- link to the live shop where appropriate.

Then show a broader store wall as supporting abundance. Do not lead with a moving wall of dozens of equally weighted cards. Six good stories create more trust than forty names without context.

### Act 7 — “What oBizee is building next”

Show the future platform as a horizon connected to the present product. The section explains how AI, advanced CRM, automation, profit intelligence, website generation, creative tools and a template marketplace extend the same operating system.

Every upcoming capability is labelled at the individual item level with its planned availability. The homepage gives priority to a few capabilities; the complete vision belongs on a dedicated page.

### Act 8 — Pricing and decision

Explain the platform charge in one sentence, then show three familiar order values. Separate platform charges from payment, shipping, domain, tax and optional service costs where applicable.

End with two clear paths:
- Start a new business/store.
- Move an existing business/store.

## Visual system

The visual identity should feel Indian through the businesses, products, language and commercial reality—not through ornamental clichés.

### Palette

- Ink: #101114
- Paper: #FFFFFF
- Soft grey: #F2F3F5
- oBizee orange: #F97316
- Deep orange for accessible light-surface actions: #C2410C
- Warm signal surface: #FFF1E8
- Future/roadmap accent: a controlled indigo used only for planned capabilities

Orange identifies oBizee. Merchant worlds introduce their own temporary colors inside previews. The overall site should not become an orange gradient from top to bottom.

### Type

Use the existing Bricolage Grotesque and Plus Jakarta Sans as the first production candidates. Instrument Serif can add editorial tension to short phrases. Typography must be evaluated with the real homepage copy and actual mobile line breaks before it is locked.

The visual hierarchy comes from scale, spacing, weight and composition. Italic serif and all-caps labels remain accents.

### Surfaces

- Light paper for explanation and trust.
- Deep ink for the connected-order and future-platform chapters.
- Product interface surfaces are crisp and legible.
- Glass is limited to overlays that genuinely sit above moving or photographic content.
- Cards should not be the default container for every idea.

### Motion

Motion reveals cause and effect:
- a product moves from catalogue into storefront;
- an order arrives;
- quantities update;
- delivery and cost information connect;
- a store changes character when the selected business changes.

Avoid decoration-only continuous motion. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Mobile uses direct selection and short transitions.

## Content standard

Every homepage line must perform one of four jobs:
- identify the visitor;
- explain an outcome;
- prove a claim;
- advance a decision.

Remove phrases such as “powerful platform,” “streamline your business,” “everything you need” and “grow effortlessly” unless the following content makes the phrase specific.

Feature names are supporting labels. Merchant outcomes lead:
- “Know what a product costs to make” before “raw-material inventory.”
- “Choose the delivery that works for this order” before “multi-courier integration.”
- “See what you kept” before “profit analytics.”

## Design workflow

I will own the first design direction inside the existing Next.js repository.

1. Verify which route currently renders the live homepage and map all dependencies.
2. Build the new homepage as an isolated, reviewable concept route or component set so the current homepage and import flow remain intact.
3. Implement the opening scene, the five-business system and the connected-order sequence at desktop and mobile widths.
4. Review the working concept visually and revise the composition, content density and interaction.
5. Present it for approval before replacing the public homepage.
6. Extend the approved language into pricing, templates, features, solutions and resources.

Claude’s useful role comes after step 3: critique the working concept as an art director, identify predictable composition or motion, and propose focused improvements. Claude should receive screenshots and the design rules, not an open request to decide the product story.

## First concept acceptance

The first concept is ready for review when:
- no single industry dominates the identity;
- each business example remains concrete;
- the first viewport communicates storefront quality and operational breadth;
- the connected order is understandable without animation;
- the current pricing advantage is visible but does not reduce the brand to cheapness;
- desktop and mobile compositions both feel deliberate;
- no unverified proof appears as fact;
- current import behavior and routes remain untouched.
